> **Context note:** This page sets out the legal questions raised by reported police conduct during the October 2026 Tirana protests, and the Albanian and international provisions that apply to them. **It states potential violations requiring independent investigation, not proven unlawful conduct.** Evidence that police used force does not by itself establish that the force was unlawful; each intervention depends on the threat involved, any warning given and the force used against each person.

# Legal framework for the October 2026 police interventions

## Why this page exists

The central legal question for the 13–15 October interventions is whether the Albanian State Police acted with legality, necessity, proportionality and accountability. The [ADCN evidence brief](../assets/ADCN_Tirana_13_October_2026_Evidence_Brief.pdf) documents reports of police restraint, a reported serious injury, tear gas and water cannon, and concerns involving journalists and detainees — and states explicitly that photographs alone cannot establish the full sequence of events or the lawfulness of individual actions.

## Issues raised by reported conduct

| Reported police conduct | Potential legal issue |
|---|---|
| Tear gas and water cannon against demonstrators | Disproportionate or unnecessary force, especially if used against peaceful participants or without adequate warning |
| Baton strikes or physical restraint | Excessive force, assault or ill-treatment if not necessary and proportionate |
| Serious injury to a bystander | Unlawful use of force and failure to protect people not involved in violence |
| Force against journalists | Interference with press freedom, potentially accompanied by ill-treatment |
| Detention of demonstrators | Arbitrary detention or procedural violations without an individual legal basis |
| Force against minors or students | Possible breach of child-protection safeguards |
| Failure to investigate credible allegations | Possible breach of the state's duty to conduct an effective, independent investigation |

These are matters requiring investigation, not established findings against any officer or unit.

## Applicable legal framework

| Instrument | Relevant provisions | What they require |
|---|---|---|
| Albanian Constitution | Article 47 | Guarantees peaceful assembly without weapons |
| Law No. 8773, 23 April 2001 ("On Demonstrations"/"On Assemblies") | Articles 1 and 3 | Protects the right to peaceful demonstration and requires police to guarantee and protect it; dispersal of an unarmed peaceful demonstration must be graduated |
| Law No. 82/2024 "On the State Police" | Articles 12 and 32 | Albanian Helsinki Committee (AHC) identifies these articles as requiring a public warning and sufficient time to disperse before force is used, subject to applicable exceptions |
| European Convention on Human Rights | Articles 2, 3, 5, 10 and 11 | Protect life; prohibit torture and inhuman or degrading treatment; protect liberty against arbitrary detention; protect freedom of expression and peaceful assembly |

Sources: [Albania peaceful assembly legal overview](https://www.rightofassembly.info/country/albania) · [Law No. 82/2024 (official, English)](https://asp.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Law-no.82-for-State-Police-26.7.2024-English.pdf) · [Albanian Helsinki Committee](https://ahc.org.al).

## Open questions about the October interventions

**Was adequate warning given before tear gas and water cannon were used?** The Albanian Helsinki Committee's July 2026 public statement raised concern that a police action near Parliament was "not preceded by a public warning" and that people were not given sufficient time to disperse voluntarily, citing Law No. 82/2024. That is a separate, earlier incident — relevant as context for how AHC applies the law, not a finding about what happened on 13 October.

**Was force directed at individuals posing a threat, or used indiscriminately?** European Court of Human Rights case law distinguishes violent participants from peaceful demonstrators: the actions of some individuals do not automatically remove the protections of assembly from everyone present.

**Did police continue using force after a person was restrained or no longer resisting?** If so, this could engage Article 3 of the Convention. The ADCN photographs raise questions that require the full video record and witness evidence to resolve, not conclusions from isolated frames.

**Were detentions individually justified and properly recorded?** Each detention should be examined for its legal basis, duration, access to a lawyer, notification of relatives and access to medical care.

## What Albanian institutions have already done

On 13 October 2026, the [Albanian Ombudsman announced](https://www.avokatipopullit.gov.al/sq/articles-layout-1/home/news/statement-by-the-ombudsman-regarding-today-s-protest-in-tirana-1379/) an *ex officio* administrative investigation into police use of force during the Tirana protest, assessed under Articles 2, 3 and 11 of the European Convention. The Albanian Helsinki Committee issued a parallel [press statement](https://ahc.org.al/deklarate-per-shtyp-komiteti-shqiptar-i-helsinkit-shpreh-shqetesim-per-dhunen-gjate-protestes-studentore-dhe-ben-thirrje-per-permbajtje-proporcionalitet-dhe-respektimin-e-te-drejtave-themelore/) the same day expressing concern about violence during the student protest and calling for restraint, proportionality and respect for fundamental rights.

This means the concerns have already reached an official Albanian oversight institution. The opening of an investigation is not itself a finding that a violation occurred, and no public report of its conclusions had been published as of this page's last review.

## What to ask for

1. An independent, incident-specific examination of police use of force, including the serious injury reported by the hospital interviewee in the ADCN brief.
2. Preservation of operational orders, body-camera and CCTV footage, detention records and relevant medical documentation, with privacy safeguards for the people involved.
3. Publication of the legal justification for deploying tear gas and water cannon, including what warnings were given and when.
4. Accountability for unlawful conduct by any participant or officer, alongside protection of peaceful assembly, journalists and bystanders.

The [UN Human Rights Committee's General Comment No. 37](https://www.ohchr.org/en/treaty-bodies/ccpr/general-comments) on the right of peaceful assembly supports timely, impartial investigations and transparent reporting of police use of force during assemblies.

We use "potential violations requiring independent investigation" rather than "proven unlawful police intervention" throughout this page, to stay consistent with this site's [editorial commitments](../editorial-policy.md).
