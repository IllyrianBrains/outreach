# Protest safety

General safety information for people attending protests in Albania, based on public reporting and established protest-safety guidance current as of 19 October 2026. This is not legal advice, and it cannot make a confrontation safe — treat it as a starting checklist. Police conduct and the situation on the ground can change quickly, so check current news and local sources before relying on anything here.

For what to buy and how to ship it, see [Help equip students in Tirana](../donate.md).

## Before you go

=== "Phone and documents"

    - Lock your phone with a passcode, not just a fingerprint or face unlock — you can be legally compelled to unlock biometrics far more easily than a passcode.
    - Install and verify [Signal ↗](https://signal.org/) for coordination, and agree on it with your group in advance.
    - Turn off Bluetooth and auto-join Wi‑Fi, and consider disabling location services, before you leave.
    - Back up photos and messages you want to keep, then decide what you are comfortable having on your phone if it is lost, seized, or searched.
    - Agree on an emergency contact and a meeting point with your group in case you are separated.

=== "What to bring"

    - Closed shoes you can move quickly in.
    - Eye protection — do not wear contact lenses; they can trap irritants against your eyes.
    - An FFP2 mask or equivalent if you expect tear gas or pepper spray.
    - Water for drinking and rinsing, and a charged power bank.
    - Cash, in case card networks or phone service are unreliable.
    - A printed note with your emergency contact and, if relevant, your blood type or any medical conditions.

=== "Know your rights in Albania"

    - Peaceful assembly is a protected right; organizers can notify authorities in advance through [e-Albania ↗](https://e-albania.al/ServiceDetails/14223).
    - If stopped: you can ask "am I free to leave?" and you have the right to remain silent and to request a lawyer.
    - If you need free legal help, contact the [Albanian Helsinki Committee's Free Legal Clinic ↗](https://ahc.org.al/en/faq/).
    - You can also report police conduct to the **People's Advocate (Ombudsman)** or raise it with the **National Chamber of Advocates**.
    - Lawyers representing protesters have themselves faced public pressure in 2026.<sup>[[9]](#references)</sup> If you witness intimidation of a lawyer, witness, or journalist, document it (time, names, badge numbers if visible) and report it to the AHC or the Ombudsman.

## During the protest

- Stay with your group and check in at your agreed meeting point if you get separated.
- Flashpoints in Tirana's 2026 protests have clustered near parliament and government buildings during confrontations — be deliberate about how close you go, especially as a crowd escalates.
- Do not pick up or touch a tear-gas canister that has just landed; it can be hot enough to burn and has caused serious hand injuries elsewhere.
- Avoid posting your real-time location publicly. If you share photos or video afterward, blur faces that aren't already public figures.
- If you are documenting the protest as press or an independent observer, carry visible identification and keep distance from police lines rather than standing between police and the crowd — journalists in Tirana have been pepper-sprayed, obstructed, and in some cases hospitalized while clearly identifiable as media.<sup>[[10]](#references)</sup>

## If you're exposed to tear gas or pepper spray

1. Move to fresh air and higher ground if you can — the gas settles low.
2. Don't rub your eyes; rubbing spreads the irritant and makes it worse.
3. Rinse your eyes with clean water or saline, and cover your nose and mouth with a wet cloth.
4. Once you're clear of the area, remove contaminated outer clothing.
5. Seek medical care for breathing difficulty, prolonged vision loss, or symptoms that don't improve. Call **112** for a serious injury, loss of consciousness, heavy bleeding, or a suspected head, spinal, or eye injury.

Claims that milk or a baking-soda mixture "neutralize" tear gas are widely repeated but disputed by medical professionals who train protest medics — treat them as unproven, not as a substitute for water, fresh air, and medical care when needed.

## If you're stopped or detained

- You have the right to remain silent and to ask whether you are free to leave.
- Ask for a lawyer. If you don't have one, the [AHC Free Legal Clinic ↗](https://ahc.org.al/en/faq/) or the National Chamber of Advocates are starting points.
- If possible, note the time, location, and any officers' names or badge numbers.
- Tell your emergency contact what happened as soon as you are able to.
- Report any mistreatment to the People's Advocate (Ombudsman) or the Albanian Helsinki Committee.

## Digital security

- Turn off location sharing and avoid live-posting your exact position.
- Blur faces in photos or video before posting them publicly, including people who did not consent to being identified.
- If you believe your accounts or devices are specifically targeted, [Access Now's Digital Security Helpline ↗](https://www.accessnow.org/help/) offers free, direct assistance.
