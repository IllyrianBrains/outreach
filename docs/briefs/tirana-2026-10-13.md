# Tirana protest — 13 October 2026

**Brief prepared:** 15 October 2026  
**Location:** Tirana, Albania  
**Status:** Preliminary media-source review; not an independent investigation  
**Use:** Journalists, civil society, public officials, diplomatic and international organizations

## Executive summary

Media outlets reported clashes during a student-led demonstration in Tirana on 13 October 2026. Vizion Plus reported that police used water and gas to disperse people near the Ministry of Education; it also described objects thrown at the building, damage and attempted entry. News24/BalkanWeb reported fires, clashes and more than 40 people taken to police stations. These are attributed media reports, not independently verified totals.

Euronews Albania reported more than 13 injuries among police, journalists, protesters and passers-by. It interviewed a seriously injured young man at the Trauma Hospital who said he had not been protesting and alleged police had harmed him. The cause of the injury has not been independently established in this brief. The same report says police suspended five officers, opened an internal review and that the Police Oversight Agency and prosecutors began investigating. Those reported investigative steps should be checked for subsequent developments.

## Questions for public authorities

1. What legal grounds and operational orders governed use of tear gas, water cannon and physical force? What warnings were given?
2. What measures protected children, students, bystanders, journalists and other non-participants?
3. Have original body-camera, CCTV, dispatch and radio records been preserved with chain of custody?
4. What are the confirmed numbers of injured and detained people, and what support and legal safeguards were provided?
5. What is the status and independence of the inquiry into the serious leg injury, and how will findings be published while protecting personal data?
6. What measures are being taken to investigate violence against officers and damage to property as well as alleged excessive police force?

## Human rights framework

Freedom of peaceful assembly and the protection of life and bodily integrity apply alongside legitimate public-safety responsibilities. The conduct of individual participants must be assessed individually. Any police use of force requires a fact-specific assessment of legality, necessity and proportionality; this brief does not reach a legal finding.
